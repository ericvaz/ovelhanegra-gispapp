Systems Design & Management: GISP practice questions

Systems Design & Management on the GISP exam covers needs assessment, architecture, capacity planning, implementation, governance, cost-benefit, procurement and system maintenance. Here are five sample questions from our bank of 40 on this category, each with the answer and a short explanation.

Question 1 · Easy · Conceptual

An organization has decided to implement a GIS. According to standard system design practice, what should be completed before any software or hardware is selected?

  1. Digitizing of the organization's existing paper maps into a common format
  2. A benchmark comparing the leading commercial and open-source desktop GIS packages
  3. A needs assessment that documents the organization's business functions, users, data, and required products
  4. Purchase of a high-capacity server so that the system can scale later
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Technology should be chosen to satisfy documented requirements, so the needs assessment comes first. Selecting software or hardware before requirements are known risks paying for capabilities nobody needs and missing the ones they do.

Question 2 · Easy · Conceptual

Which cost items are included in the total cost of ownership (TCO) of a GIS?

  1. Only recurring annual subscription and maintenance fees
  2. Initial acquisition costs plus ongoing costs such as maintenance, support, training, staffing, data, and eventual upgrades over the system's life
  3. The projected revenue the system will generate minus the purchase price
  4. Only the purchase price of software licences and hardware
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. TCO captures the full lifetime cost of owning and operating a system, not just the initial purchase. Recurring costs such as staff, support, training and upgrades usually exceed the initial outlay over a multi-year life.

Question 3 · Medium · Conceptual

Which body developed the Content Standard for Digital Geospatial Metadata (CSDGM) used widely by U.S. government agencies?

  1. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
  2. The World Wide Web Consortium (W3C)
  3. The Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC)
  4. The Federal Geographic Data Committee (FGDC)
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. The FGDC published the CSDGM in 1994 and revised it in 1998; it was mandated for U.S. federal geospatial data and FGDC now encourages transition to ISO 19115-based metadata. OGC develops service and encoding standards, not the CSDGM.

Question 4 · Medium · Scenario

A municipal GIS coordinator proposes a six-month pilot in one department before an enterprise rollout. What is the primary purpose of such a pilot?

  1. To replace a formal needs assessment, since real use reveals requirements faster
  2. To test the design, data, and workflows at limited scale and risk so that lessons can be applied before full deployment
  3. To demonstrate the system to the vendor so that licence costs can be renegotiated
  4. To train every end user in the organization before the production system exists
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A pilot validates the design and surfaces problems while the cost of change is still low. It complements rather than replaces the needs assessment, and it is deliberately limited in scope and users.

Question 5 · Hard · Conceptual

Which statement best distinguishes data governance from day-to-day data management?

  1. Governance sets the policies, decision rights, standards, and accountability for data; management executes those policies through operational tasks such as loading, editing, and backup
  2. Governance is a one-time activity during implementation, while management continues afterward
  3. Governance concerns spatial data only, while management concerns attribute data
  4. Governance is performed only by database administrators, while management is performed by executives
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Governance answers who decides and by what rules; management does the work within those rules. Governance is an ongoing, cross-functional responsibility typically led by business owners and stewards rather than by DBAs alone.

What this category tests

Systems Design & Management is one of the ten knowledge categories in the official GISCI exam blueprint. Questions are vendor-neutral: they test concepts and professional judgement rather than the menus of one software package, and they come as conceptual, application, scenario and data-interpretation items. Expect the exam to mix easy recall with harder scenario questions where you must choose the best course of action.
